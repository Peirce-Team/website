Peirce Interprets Peirce

A collaborative digital humanities project dedicated to exploring the original manuscripts of Charles S. Peirce.
By combining rigorous semiotic scholarship with state-of-the-art computational methods, we aim to make Peirce’s intellectual legacy more accessible, analyzable, and engaging for scholars and the wider public.

Discover

Why Peirce, Why Now

Charles S. Peirce, a foundational thinker in semiotics, logic, and the philosophy of science, left a vast and intricate archive of manuscripts. Many remain underexplored due to their scope and complexity.

Today’s advances in OCR/HTR, NLP, and knowledge graph technologies offer a timely opportunity to revisit this archive and reveal new insights into Peirce’s concepts—such as icons, indexes, symbols, and the universal categories of Firstness, Secondness, and Thirdness.

Objectives & Methods

We aim to build a curated, high-fidelity digital corpus of Peirce’s manuscripts and analyze linguistic, conceptual, and diagrammatic patterns using AI-driven methods.

Approach:

  • Transcription: Advanced OCR/HTR for accurate digital texts.
  • Analysis: NLP and LLMs to detect themes and semiotic structures.
  • Knowledge Graph: Structuring insights for cross-referencing.
  • Visualization: Interactive visual summaries.

Davide Picca is a professor and researcher in digital humanities and artificial intelligence at the University of Lausanne. His work focuses on the intersection between language, semiotics and AI, based on the principle that linguistic models are not mere computer machines, but genuine tools for interpreting culture and thought. During his career, he has been a visiting professor at the University of Turin and the Sorbonne in Paris, and is currently a research fellow at Harvard University's metaLAB, where he studies the relationship between digital technologies and the humanities. He is also a scientific member of the Umberto Eco Foundation, where he coordinates aspects related to technology and artificial intelligence, with the aim of improving archives, libraries and collections through advanced digital tools.

Irene Mittelberg was a professor and internationally recognised expert in cognitive linguistics and cognitive semiotics at the Institute of English Studies at RWTH Aachen University. Among her many endeavours, she led the DFG-funded research project “NeuroPeirce: Gesture as test ground for the social neuroscience of Peirce’s Universal Categories“, where she developed and helped implement an adapted scheme of Peirce’s universal categories for coding and analysing both naturalistic social interaction and the corresponding brain-imaging data. Irene brought to the team an extensive knowledge of semiotic theories and concepts and their relevance for empirical applications. She was well-connected to the world of Peirce studies and semiotics through her many affiliations to and advisory roles in academic societies such as the German Society for Semiotics. She is greatly missed by all who had the privilege of working with her.

Alessandro Adamou is Digital Humanities Scientist at the Bibliotheca Hertziana - Max Planck Institute for Art History. His work focuses on applications of Knowledge graphs and the Semantic Web to understand and tackle the research questions of digital humanists. His other core research interests revolve around the structuring and knowledge organization of intangible aspects of cultural heritage, including ways to model aesthetic experience, symbols in tradition, and hermeneutics. With the Royal College of Music, Alessandro has developed LED, the first crowdsourced linked dataset on evidence of listening experiences; he has also contributed knowledge models of reading experience for the READ-IT project. At the Bibliotheca Hertziana, Alessandro is in charge of the knowledge graph infrastructure of the institutional holdings in art history, including their usage in machine learning and generative AI applications. He was PMC member of the project Stanbol at the Apache Software Foundation and organizer of the WHiSe workshop series, and currently serves in the editorial board of Applied Ontology and, since 2023, as chair at the ACM Hypertext conference.

Sebastian Feil is a post-doctoral researcher at RWTH Aachen with the department of English Studies in the research area Linguistics and Cognitive Semiotics. He hold a Master’s degree in Comparative Literature, American Literature and Philosophy from the University of Augsburg and has completed a PhD in Philosophy with Prof. Helmut Pape at the University of Hildesheim on the concept of the preconceptual with particular attention to American Pragmatism and the concept of habit in the epistemology and semiotics of Charles Sanders Peirce. He has worked on the relationship of Peirce’s philosophy and semiotics and contemporary continental philosophy (Gadamer, Foucault, Blumenberg) and Peirce’s concept of abduction. Sebastian Feil serves as a member of the Advisory Committee of the German Semiotics Society (DGS).

Carlo Teo Pedretti is a PhD candidate in Heritage Science at La Sapienza University of Rome, and holds an MA in Philosophical Sciences and Digital Humanities from the University of Bologna. His research applies semantic web technologies to model scholarly hermeneutical processes, focusing on the act of annotation. Currently, he uses visual semiotics and VLMs to interpret diagrams in historical manuscripts.

Lorenzo Zangari earned his Ph.D. in Information and Communication Technologies in February 2025 at the Department of Computer Engineering, Modeling, Electronics, and Systems Engineering (DIMES), University of Calabria, Italy. He graduated magna cum laude in Computer Engineering in 2020. His research interests include the analysis and design of deep representation learning methods for complex networks and natural language processing (NLP). He is currently a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Lausanne (UNIL).
